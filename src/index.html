Quito, Ecuador

The Historic Andean Capital in the Shadow of Pichincha Volcano

Discover Quito

Discover the Middle of the World

Perched high in the Andes mountains at an elevation of 2,850 meters, Quito is the closest capital city to the equator. Surrounded by dramatic volcanic peaks and home to the best-preserved historic center in the Americas, it offers an unforgettable blend of natural wonder and cultural legacy.

High Altitude Tips (2,850m+)

Stay hydrated, take it easy on your first day, and wear sun protection—the equatorial sun at high altitudes is intense! If you plan to hike Rucu Pichincha from TelefériQo, start early in the morning before mountain clouds roll in.

Iconic Destinations

Explore three must-visit places in Quito, from towering peaks to historic plazas and urban parks.

TelefériQo Quito

One of the highest cable cars in the world, taking you up to Cruz Loma on Pichincha at 3,945m.

Historic Center & Churches

Magnificent colonial architecture including the iconic Basílica del Voto Nacional and San Francisco.

Parque Metropolitano

Nearly 1,400 acres of peaceful eucalyptus trails offering sweeping views of the Andean valleys.

Quito at a Glance

Discover key facts about Ecuador's highland capital nestled against the volcanic Andes.

2,850m Elevation

The second-highest official capital city in the world, sitting right in the Guayllabamba river basin in the northern Andes.

Eternal Spring

Thanks to its equatorial altitude, Quito enjoys steady daytime temperatures between 18°C and 22°C all 365 days of the year.

1st UNESCO Site

In 1978, UNESCO declared Quito the very first World Cultural Heritage city alongside Krakow, Poland for its preservation.

Pichincha Volcano

Its active stratovolcano neighbor features two summits: Rucu (4,698m) and Guagua (4,784m), visible from across the city.
